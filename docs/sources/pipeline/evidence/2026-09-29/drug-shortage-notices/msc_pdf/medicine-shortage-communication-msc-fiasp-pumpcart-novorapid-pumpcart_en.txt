Fiasp 100 units/mL PumpCart solution for injection in cartridge 
(insulin aspart) and NovoRapid PumpCart 100 units/mL solution for 
injection in cartridge (insulin aspart): Supply shortage and cessation 
of marketing for Fiasp Pumpcart 

Dear Healthcare Professional, 

Novo Nordisk A/S [affiliate to include country specific information to reflect global or local company 

name] is notifying healthcare professionals about potential or ongoing shortages of Fiasp PumpCart 

and NovoRapid PumpCart  

Overview of situation 

•  Current manufacturing capacity cannot meet the demand for Fiasp 100 

U/mL PumpCart solution for injection in cartridge and NovoRapid 
PumpCart 100 U/mL solution for injection in cartridge. This has led, or 
may lead to intermittent shortages throughout 2025. 

•  The shortages are not related to a quality defect or an efficacy or safety 

issue. 

•  In addition, due to commercial reasons, marketing of Fiasp PumpCart 

will cease by the end of 2026 at the latest in all EU/EEA countries where 
the product is currently marketed. 

Mitigation measures 

In order to manage the shortage, Novo Nordisk is engaging with the European Medicines Agency and 
the [National Competent Authority] on mitigation measures.  

The product shortage could result in patients missing the required doses, which may lead to serious 

clinical consequences, specifically hyperglycaemia that may eventually progress to diabetic 
ketoacidosis. 

Healthcare professionals (HCPs) should consider the following mitigation measures:  

•  No new patients should be started on Fiasp PumpCart or NovoRapid PumpCart throughout 2025.  

• 

In case Fiasp PumpCart or NovoRapid PumpCart is unavailable, healthcare professionals should 

switch existing patients to an alternative fast-acting insulin therapy (such as Fiasp, NovoRapid or 

its insulin aspart biosimilars, fast-acting insulin lispro or its biosimilars, insulin glulisine or its 

biosimilars, insulin lispro or its biosimilars), based on existing guidance and clinical judgment. In 

case a switch from the current therapy is needed, healthcare professionals should ensure that all 

patients are counselled on any changes and provided with adequate training, if needed. 

Background information 

•
